[[redacted]] said something that made me think: something something… dubai airways… something about their flight attendants looking similar to eachother, no individualism.. and yea.

i kept picturing it. a whole crew of people smoothed down until nothing sticks out. it's their job, and the sameness is the point. you're meant to get the exact same experience on your first flight as on your fiftieth, and that only works if the person handing you the hot towel is interchangeable.

what bothered me was that the image felt familiar.

we talk about individualism all the time. find your voice, do your own thing, don't follow the crowd. then you open instagram or tiktok and everyone is saying the same phrases, using the same sounds, posing in front of the same view. and hey dont we all? 

even the people who look different usually look different in a recognisable way. there's a uniform for being alternative, just like there's one for being mainstream. you pick your group, learn the look, learn the lines. it feels like choosing, but most of the time you're choosing off a menu someone else wrote.

i asked myself something similar when i was thinking about substances, and about technology: are we actually getting the thing we're chasing? we go looking for uniqueness through what we wear and post and buy, and end up as our own little cabin crew, dressed for our subculture, saying our script, convinced we're standing apart.

real individualism is quieter than that, and harder. it's less about how you look and more about what's left when you take away the feed, your friends' opinions, the path everyone assumes you'll take. what do you actually care about then? would you still want the thing if nobody could see you having it? sometimes it means holding an opinion that doesn't fit your group. it means picking a direction because it matters to you, not because it makes a good story.

the flight attendants at least get paid for it. the rest of us do it for free, mostly without noticing.

so maybe individualism isn't something you show. maybe, like happiness, or like coming to terms with where you started, it isn't something you can chase head-on. it's just what's left once you stop letting other people's scripts decide how you look, think, and live.
