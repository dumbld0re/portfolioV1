---
title: "consumerism and the youth"
date: "2026-10-01"
summary: "the thing you bought was never really the thing you wanted."
---


i've been thinking about how much of being young right now is spent buying things. or wanting to buy things. or watching other people buy things.

it doesn't feel like consumerism when you're inside it. it feels like taste. you're not buying a pair of shoes, you're buying the pair that says something about you. you're not buying a coffee, you're buying the café, the cup, the photo of the cup. the object is almost the least important part.

we're told to be ourselves, and then sold the way to do it. every group has a shopping list. the alternative kid and the mainstream kid are both buying an identity, just from different shelves.

and the shelves are everywhere now. you used to have to go somewhere to be sold to. now the shop is in your pocket and it never closes. the feed is a shop window. half the people you follow are selling something, and the other half are selling themselves, which usually means selling something.

what's strange is how much of it isn't even buying. it's watching. hauls, unboxings, "everything i bought this month", "get ready with me". we watch other people consume. it's living through other people's experiences on a screen, except here the experience is literally a purchase.

buying something gives you a little hit. if i'm honest, the wanting is usually better than the having. the scrolling, the comparing, the waiting for it to arrive. then it arrives and the feeling is gone in a day or two. i felt like this for my new phone. saved up for a couple of months, waiting a couple of weeks, and then i got it. it was nice sure, but okay now what? its a phone.

the funny thing is that the best part of the phone happened before i had it. the saving was the part with meaning in it. every month i put money away, i was choosing it over something else. i was working toward something. then the waiting, checking the delivery, imagining what it would be like. for a few months that phone was a goal. the second it was in my hand, it stopped being a goal and became a thing, and a thing just sits there.

i think that's what the whole system runs on. not the having, the wanting. if the having actually satisfied us, we'd stop. so the having has to wear off, and something has to be waiting to replace it.

and with phones, something always is. a new one comes out every year. the one i saved months for will look old in about twelve months, not because anything about it changed, but because something newer exists. nothing breaks. it just gets made to feel behind. that's a strange thing to pay for: a feeling with an expiry date built in.

it's even stranger when you think about what the phone actually is. the thing i saved up for is the same thing that shows me the ads, the hauls, the next phone. i paid for the shop window. i carry it everywhere. and it's very good at making me want things i didn't know existed an hour ago.

that's what i think is different about being young now. the selling isn't loud anymore. it doesn't feel like an advert. it feels like a recommendation, like content, like something you found yourself. the algorithm learns what you like, then shows you a version of it that costs money. you think you're discovering your taste. a lot of the time you're being handed it.

the object stays, but the feeling leaves, so you go looking for the next one. it works a lot like tolerance. you adapt, the effect gets smaller, and you need a bit more to feel the same.

the thing you bought was never really the thing you wanted.

i do understand why people want nice things. when you've had very little, buying something nice can feel like proof that you're okay now. like you made it out. and when you've always had a lot, buying things can just become normal, something you do without thinking about whether it's possible. i'm honestly not sure which is sadder: buying things to prove you've made it, or buying things because you've never had to think about it.

i don't want this to sound like i'm against buying things. i'm not. some things are worth it. a camera you actually use, a book you actually read, a meal you'll remember (i spend so much money on food its not even a joke) . even the phone is fine. i use it every day. the problem was never the phone, it was expecting the phone to feel like more than a phone.

the question i keep coming back to is simple: does this thing let me do something, or does it just let me be seen having it?

would you still want it if nobody could see you having it?

maybe the better question isn't "what does this do?", but "what am i hoping this will do for me?" if i want to belong, buying the clothes won't make anyone care about me. if i want to feel like my life is moving, buying new things for it isn't the same as moving it.

and maybe the phone taught me something without meaning to. the months of saving were better than the phone. the goal was better than the object. if that's true, then maybe what i actually want isn't more things, it's more things to work toward.

maybe consumerism is just the most socially acceptable shortcut there is. 

anyway, i cant wait to buy more things!
